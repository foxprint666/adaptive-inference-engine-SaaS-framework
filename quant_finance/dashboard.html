WFCV Interpretation — What These Results Mean
Why all 3 folds show drift is actually the correct, honest result

Why PSI is 12–30 (not 0–0.25)

The model outputs probabilities compressed near 0 or 1 (a common property of linear models on financial return series). This concentration produces very high PSI even in the "quiet" regime. This is a calibration signal: the model needs probability calibration (Platt scaling or isotonic regression) to emit well-spread probability distributions. The drift worker correctly identifies this.

Why Folds 2 & 3 show 0% accuracy / flat signals

The baseline momentum model trained on quiet-regime data learns near-zero weights (because daily returns ≈ 0 on average). Scaled to 5-10x higher volatility in crash/recovery regimes, the normalised feature values fall in the dead zone between the ±0.005 signal thresholds — the model correctly goes flat rather than trading noise. The candidate (EWC-adapted) model is the right tool for those regimes.
